Text Busta

For a phone without email. Busta texts you when mail lands in a folder you've switched Text me on, with a link that opens that one email in any browser, once. You can also text Busta a question, like "what time and place are my kids' soccer games this weekend?". It searches all your accounts, reads the emails and their attachments, and texts back a short answer laid out for a small screen, plus a one-time link to a page with the details and the emails it used. Texting runs through a Twilio number registered to you. The texts, numbers and answers here are examples; the folders and accounts are yours. v4 added a threat model (the Security tab). v5: links need your PIN and last 12 hours, and each texting feature is off until you turn it on and confirm its risk. v6: sender and subject are opt-in (off by default), and phones without a browser get texts without links. v7: every feature, search included, is opt-in, and you choose which folders text you.

What a notification says

By default only the folder and a link: New email in Must read busta.app/v/Xk3pQ9. Which folders text you is your choice, from your own folders; nothing is chosen by default. Sender and subject are opt-in (off by default) because the lock screen shows them to anyone holding the phone: Must read · Liz Taylor: Dinner Sunday? busta.app/v/…. Never body text. Security mail and mail you filed by hand never text.

As it happens, one text per email

An important email texts you as soon as it's filed, with its own link. No nightly summary, no quiet hours and no bundling for now. A safety cap of 20 texts an hour stops a runaway rule from flooding your phone; past it, one text says "More in Must read" instead.

Links open one thing, once

Each link is a random token for one message, good for 12 hours and one open, and it needs your 4-digit Busta PIN. It lands on a PIN page first, so a link checker fetching it doesn't use it up; 5 wrong PINs kill the link. Someone you forward the text to could open it only if you also give them the PIN, a risk you've accepted. Opening starts a 30-minute session for that message only: reload, attachments, nothing else. After that it says "used" and offers a new one by text. An answer link works the same way, and its session reaches the answer page and only the emails it cites.

Needs a yes

Answers for a small screen

The text is the answer, not a paragraph: a heading, then short lines (when, where, who) of 20 characters or less, the width of a flip phone's screen, so nothing wraps mid-thought. Under 160 characters when it can be, never more than 2 texts. Then More: and a one-time link to the answer page, which has the full answer, the lines it relied on quoted from each email, and a way to open each of those emails. Only your verified number can ask; it searches all your accounts; if it isn't sure it says so. About 1–2¢ a question, 30 a day.

Finding the answer: cheap first, expensive last

Every conversation is already its own Durable Object, so the question fans out to them. 1. A small model plans it: dates, names, and keywords with synonyms ("games" also means match, schedule, fixture). 2. Busta's search gathers candidates across your accounts, plus the last 14 days. 3. Each candidate conversation scores its own messages with jev, the model that already sorts your mail, using the question as the "rule", all in parallel. 4. Only the top 5 are read in full, attachments too, and go to the expensive reasoning model. No vector index to build or keep in sync.

Your phone, verified by Twilio Verify

You type your number in Texting settings, and Twilio Verify texts a 6-digit code. It expires in 10 minutes, with 3 tries and a resend timer. The number is stored on your user, and a reverse lookup routes incoming texts to you, one user per number. Changing it needs a new code. Verify uses Twilio's own senders, so this works before Busta's number is approved.

Off until you opt in

Verifying a phone turns nothing on. Next you set a PIN, then switch on each feature separately: texts about new mail (for folders you pick; none are picked for you), sender and subject, links to open emails, and Ask by text, which searches your mail. Each one lists what it risks and needs a tick that you understand before it turns on. Turning one off takes effect immediately; STOP turns all of them off.

Phones without a browser

Some dumb phones (a Light Phone, say) can't open links. Leave Open emails from a link off and texts come without links; answers then get up to 3 texts so they stand on their own. Links can still be opened later on a computer if you turn them on. Most other dumb phones run a form of Android with a basic browser, which is enough for the PIN page.

Needs a yes

Security first (see the Security tab)

A threat model found that texting ties your mail to a phone number instead of a device, so a SIM swap or a recycled number could read your mail. Required before shipping: a PIN on links, security mail never texted or searched, link scope enforced in code, periodic re-verification with a SIM-swap check, Twilio body redaction, and cost caps.

Needs a yes

Twilio registration

US texting from an app needs A2P 10DLC registration. Without a business tax ID it's Sole Proprietor: your name and address, a code to your phone, one number. That's enough to text you. Texting other Busta users later would need a business registration. STOP and HELP work as the carriers require.
