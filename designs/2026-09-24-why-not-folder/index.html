Why Not This Folder

When a message doesn't land in the folder you expected, Busta shows why and helps you fix the rule. It points at the sentences in your rule that nearly matched and the words in the email that mattered. Then it suggests keywords to add, and before you save it shows the new score for this message and any other mail the change would pull in. A Tidy up button also rewrites a rule that has grown into word soup as a short, readable list, keeping what it means. The example is your real one: "Bergamo" scored 35% for Must read and stayed in Messages.

Scores and reasons come from different places

The percentages are the real scores from the model that files your mail (typesafe/jev). It only gives numbers, so the reasons come from a second Workers AI model. That model reads the same email and your rule, split into sentences, and returns JSON: a verdict and a one-line reason for each sentence, the words it relied on, and suggested keywords. It never changes a score.

Only when you ask

Nothing is explained until you press Why not Must read?, so there's no extra AI cost per message. The answer is saved on the message until the rule changes. It works for "No folder" and for "Unsure" messages, and you can ask about any folder, not just the top guess.

Check before you save

Check it re-scores this message with the new rule, and also your last 20 messages. That way you see what else would move into the folder. Broad phrases like "notes I send myself" can pull in more than you meant, and the check shows exactly which messages.

What saving does

The sentence is added to the end of the folder's rule, never rewriting what you wrote. This message and your recent mail are re-sorted with the folder editor's default window (7 days, up to 100). Messages you filed yourself don't move. Undo restores the previous rule. Just file this one moves only this message and leaves the rule alone.

Tidy up keeps the meaning

The same text model rewrites the rule as "Goes in" and "Leaves out" lists, fixing grammar and merging repeats. It's told to keep every condition and add none. Before you use it, both versions score your last 20 messages, and any message whose result would change is listed. Your version is kept for Undo.

Colors keep their meaning

Amber dashes mark the sentences that nearly matched, and blue marks the words in the email that mattered. Your own address is amber because it's the catch. Green appears only for "would go in" and for the text being added.
